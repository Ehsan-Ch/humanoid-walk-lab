What the robot learns

  1. Start with a hand-designed controller structure: a periodic leg motion and balance feedback.
  2. Sample 48 candidate sets of 16 parameters per generation. Simulate each candidate twice for up to six seconds.
  3. Use the best candidates to update the search distribution. Repeat for 50 generations.
  4. Freeze the winning parameters, then evaluate new seeds for twelve seconds and apply separate stress conditions.

The cross-entropy method learns the frequency, joint motion coefficients, and balance gains. The physics engine computes movement from joint torques and contact forces; forward position is not scripted.

Where it still fails

The learned controller handles these normal and low-friction trials, but the backward push causes falls. A high score in this simplified environment does not imply robust humanoid locomotion.

Only one optimizer seed was trained. Initial disturbances are small, arms are fixed, self-collisions are disabled, and there is no lateral motion, uneven terrain, perception, or hardware validation. Some foot sliding is possible; alternating contact checks do not measure slip-free walking.
